# C-003 — Higgsfield narrative treatment: “A Course Appears”

Status: Brand-approved concept; Founder publish approval required

## Narrative intent

Show the delight of turning an ordinary pen and a printed page into a tiny round of golf. The story should feel tactile, handmade, playful, and calm—not glossy, futuristic, or overproduced.

## Format

- 30 seconds
- Vertical 9:16 master, center-safe for 1:1 crop
- Natural paper-and-ink sound bed with a restrained, upbeat acoustic pulse
- Use the real PPG cover and demo footage as product-reference inputs

## Shot sequence

1. 0:00–0:04 — Macro overhead of warm, textured paper on a tabletop. A watercolor fairway gently resolves into view. Slow push-in. Voiceover: “What if a round of golf could begin on a blank page?”
2. 0:04–0:08 — A real pen enters frame and stands upright at the tee. Subtle parallax only. On-screen: “Stand it.”
3. 0:08–0:12 — Match the real demo’s hand position and flick action. Use the real motion as reference; do not invent a different mechanic. On-screen: “Flick it.”
4. 0:12–0:16 — The pen lands. A small pencil mark appears exactly where it touches. Macro rack focus. On-screen: “Mark it.”
5. 0:16–0:22 — Three quick tabletop vignettes: tee, fairway obstacle, and flag. Watercolor edges breathe subtly; the printed product itself remains unchanged. Voiceover: “Every landing is a stroke. Lowest score wins.”
6. 0:22–0:26 — Two players’ hands share the page; a score is marked. Keep people secondary and product interaction clear. Voiceover: “Quick to learn. Ready almost anywhere.”
7. 0:26–0:30 — Resolve to the real current cover art on the paper-textured background. On-screen: “Pen Pad Golf” then “A little golf. A lot of fun.” End CTA: “See how to play.”

## Higgsfield direction

Create a tactile tabletop product film using the supplied Pen Pad Golf cover art and real demonstration footage as strict visual and motion references. Warm daylight, watercolor course illustration, printed-paper texture, deep ink green, olive, muted gold, and restrained rust accents. Camera language: macro overhead, slow push-in, gentle rack focus, subtle parallax, and clean match cuts. Preserve the actual product artwork, wording, proportions, pen-flick mechanic, and logo. Make all generated environmental frames clearly supportive of the real product rather than replacements for it.

## Negative constraints

- No photoreal golf course replacing the printed game
- No altered logo, cover wording, colors, or invented packaging
- No golf club, ball, dice, phone, app interface, or electronic gameplay
- No impossible pen physics or exaggerated impact
- No availability, shipping, durability, age-range, or testimonial claims
- No neon palette, chrome, sci-fi lighting, luxury-car aesthetic, or loud sports-broadcast graphics
- No generated close-up that could be mistaken for a different physical product

## Brand anchors

- Headline: Playfair Display
- Functional copy: DM Sans
- Ink `#193225`, green `#31543B`, olive `#667A47`, paper `#F7F1E4`, gold `#9B7319`, rust `#9C3F2C`
- Approved lines: “A little golf. A lot of fun.” and “Stand it. Flick it. Mark it.”

## Success criteria

The viewer understands the mechanic without explanation, recognizes the real product, and has one next action: see how to play.
